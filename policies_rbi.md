# RBI Regulatory Reference (External)

Paraphrased summaries of real Reserve Bank of India regulatory instruments, kept
separate from the bank's own internal policy manual (`policies.md`). The memo
should distinguish "this is what the regulator requires" from "this is what our
credit policy adds on top of that requirement." Where the two overlap, the
internal policy is generally the stricter of the two, as it should be.

## RBI-001 Fair Practices Code for Lenders
Originally issued 2003, most recently reinforced via the RBI Fair Practices Code
circulars (RBI/2015-16/16) and the 2026 Fourth Amendment Directions on
Responsible Business Conduct. Requires every regulated lender to give a written,
specific reason for rejecting a loan application — a generic or vague decline is
itself a compliance failure, independent of whether the underlying decision was
sound. Also requires transparent disclosure of all fees and charges before
disbursement, and prohibits coercive or misleading recovery conduct.

## RBI-002 Recovery Conduct (Responsible Business Conduct Directions, 2026)
The 2026 amendment converts fair-recovery expectations into enforceable rules.
Recovery agents may contact borrowers only between 8 AM and 7 PM, may not visit
during personal events or public holidays, and may not contact a borrower's
family, employer, or associates about the debt without consent. The lender, not
the recovery agent, remains legally responsible for how recovery is conducted,
and must maintain records of every agent interaction.

## RBI-003 Risk Weights on Unsecured Consumer Credit
Per RBI's November 2023 revision (partially eased by the February 2025
notifications for microfinance and certain NBFC exposures), unsecured consumer
credit — personal loans excluding housing, education, vehicle, and gold-backed
loans — carries a risk weight of 125% for both banks and NBFCs. Credit card
receivables carry 150% for banks and 125% for NBFCs. This is a capital-adequacy
rule, not an underwriting rule: it does not forbid an unsecured loan, but it
raises the capital the lender must hold against it, which is why internal policy
(POL-006) caps single-borrower unsecured exposure well below what capital rules
alone would allow.

## RBI-004 Income Recognition and Asset Classification (IRAC) Norms
An advance becomes a Non-Performing Asset when interest or principal remains
overdue for more than 90 days. This 90-day threshold is a regulatory floor, not
a target — it defines when an account *must* be classified as non-performing for
reporting and provisioning purposes. Internal policy treating any account
currently 90+ days past due as an automatic decline (POL-005) is consistent with,
and stricter than, this regulatory minimum.

## RBI-005 KYC Master Direction
Requires verified customer identification and address proof before establishing
any lending relationship, updated periodically (most recently reflected in the
Master Direction on KYC). Income and bank statement documentation must be
current — not older than a specified validity window — before a credit decision
is finalized. Internal policy's three-month income-proof and six-month
bank-statement windows (POL-007) implement this requirement at the bank's own,
more conservative cadence.

## RBI-006 Digital Lending Guidelines (2022, updated 2026)
Applies to digital/app-based lending channels. Requires a standardized Key Fact
Statement (KFS) disclosing the Annual Percentage Rate, recovery mechanism, and a
mandatory cooling-off period before the loan can be exited penalty-free — at
least three days for loans of seven days or longer tenor. Credit limits cannot
be increased without the borrower's explicit, per-instance consent. Not
applicable to this manual/branch-channel workflow unless the bank later offers
this product digitally.

## RBI-007 Responsible Lending — Interest Application Practices
RBI has directed lenders to charge interest only from the actual date of fund
disbursement, not from the date of sanction or loan-agreement execution, and not
from the date of a disbursement cheque if the borrower received it later. A
scorecard or memo that computes EMI or interest from any date earlier than
actual disbursement would itself be a fair-practices issue, independent of the
credit decision's merits.
